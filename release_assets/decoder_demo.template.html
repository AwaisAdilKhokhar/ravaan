One of them stops improving. The other doesn't.

Bits per byte on held-out Urdu — lower is better — against how many times each model has seen the corpus. The autoregressive model bottoms out at 4 epochs and then climbs: more training makes it worse. The diffusion model was trained sixteen times longer than that and had still not turned. The released pair is each arm's own best checkpoint, which is why the AR half is a 4-epoch model and says so.

What this page is not

The caveats, up front.

  • bound The diffusion number is an ELBO — an upper bound on its true bits per byte; the AR number is exact. That asymmetry means a bound can establish a diffusion win and never an AR one. Here it happens to work in diffusion's favour, and it would be worth nothing if it went the other way.
  • seeds One seed per arm. The diffusion arm was replicated separately at a second seed and landed 0.29σ away, so its own number is stable — but the gap between the arms is a single paired comparison, with no between-seed interval behind it.
  • prompts The prompts on this page are hand-written, not drawn from the held-out split. They are short because short sentences are readable; they were chosen by someone who knows what the model does well, and that is a selection effect worth naming.
  • samples Each sample is the best of 16 seeds per arm, picked by a fixed rule — fewest repeated words, then highest type/token ratio — applied identically to both arms. Every rejected draw ships in reports/demo_trace_pool.json with the reason it lost.
  • scale 70M parameters on 85M tokens. This is a controlled comparison between two factorizations, not a usable Urdu assistant. Read the samples as evidence about training dynamics, not as a product.
  • judgement No native speakers were recruited to score these. Every Urdu quality judgement in this project is a model's or the author's, including the filter that chose what you see.
