A diffusion model that reads Urdu better than its autoregressive twin

To our knowledge, the first masked diffusion language model trained for Urdu. Released alongside a matched autoregressive baseline — same corpus, same architecture, same budget — so the comparison can be checked rather than believed.

0.7659 bits​/byte  vs  0.7774 autoregressive Held-out native Urdu, lower is better. Margin +0.0115 bpb = 6.4 sem over nine independent evaluation draws.
The result

One objective could spend the compute. The other couldn’t.

The diffusion figure is an ELBO — an upper bound, averaged over nine seeded draws. That asymmetry runs in this result’s favour and is worth stating plainly: a bound that falls below an exact likelihood proves the bound’s model is better, but the reverse would prove nothing. This comparison could establish a diffusion win and could never have established an autoregressive one.

And it is faster

160 sequential steps, or 8 parallel ones

An autoregressive model writes 160 tokens in 160 forward passes, each waiting on the last. The diffusion model fills the whole canvas in 8 denoising passes. Measured over 12 matched prompts on the same laptop GPU, that is a median of 0.37 s against 6.59 s — 17.9× faster.

The step count is not a preference, it is part of the model. More denoising steps decode worse here, monotonically: 8 steps score 0.016 on repetition where 160 score 0.084, and the confidence schedule at any step count collapses into looping a single phrase. The better setting is also the cheap one, which is not how these trade-offs usually go.

What this is not

The limits, stated rather than buried

The Urdu was never read by a native speaker

Every judgement of fluency in this project was made by a language model. A human evaluation was designed and specified, and never ran — the annotators were never recruited. Treat the qualitative claims as unvalidated.

One seed per arm

There is no estimate of seed-to-seed variance on this pair, so the gap cannot be separated from a single initialization draw. The error bar above is the evaluation estimator’s, not the training run’s.

A known lexical defect is in the training data

The Roman-Urdu source renders eight common words as fixed unrelated words, in 2.77% of rows after deduplication. It was disclosed rather than filtered, because filtering the confirmed cases cannot reach the unscreened tail without the native-speaker pass that never happened.

Not the discovery, the replication

The data-constrained crossover itself is prior work — Prabhudesai et al. (arXiv:2507.15857) and Ni et al. (arXiv:2511.03276), both on English and code. What is new here is the language and the script: naturally low-resource, non-Latin, noisy web text. Nor is this the first diffusion model for a non-English language; Diffutron (arXiv:2603.20466) is a Turkish one.

70M parameters, and no tuning of any kind

No instruction tuning, no alignment, no safety filtering. It produces fluent-sounding false statements and reproduces the biases of filtered web text. It is a research artifact, not a product.

Provenance

Six training runs, about $28

The corpus freeze — ten stages of filtering, deduplication, PII screening and held-out decontamination — ran entirely on free tiers and a laptop GPU. The released diffusion model is 64 epochs on one rented RTX 5090: 5.46B tokens, 8.2 hours, roughly $4.60. The design was preregistered before any training, with four falsifiable predictions and a deviation log recording every departure.
